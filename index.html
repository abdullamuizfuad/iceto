ICETO Gelato: A Flagship in Singapore, a Freezer in Every 7-Eleven

1. From Majeedhee Magu to the region

ICETO opened in 2019 as a small gelato counter on Majeedhee Magu in Malé. Its three founders each brought a different skill. Yusuf trained as a food scientist in Italy, Aishath studied pastry in Singapore, and Hassan spent ten years managing food and beverage at Maldivian resorts. Together they built a brand on three ideas: real Italian gelato made with slow-churn machines, bold flavours using Maldivian ingredients such as young coconut, pandan, tamarind, mango and toddy palm syrup, and a bright, photo-friendly style that made ICETO popular on Instagram.

Six years later, ICETO runs five outlets in Malé, Hulhumalé and Addu City, plus two counters inside resorts. All gelato is made in one central kitchen in Hulhumalé. In 2025 the company earned MVR 19 million in revenue with a gross margin of 48 percent. It has no debt, but most profits have been spent on opening new outlets, so cash for a large overseas project is limited. None of the founders has run a business outside the Maldives before.

2. Why grow abroad, and why now?

By 2025, growth in the Maldives was slowing. Almost every busy street in Malé and Hulhumalé already had an ICETO outlet or a competitor nearby, and the local population is small. The founders listed their options. They could sell more to existing customers through loyalty offers and delivery apps. They could create new products for the Maldives, such as gelato cakes and packaged pints for supermarkets. They could start a completely different business, such as a bakery-café chain. Or they could take the existing gelato range into new markets overseas. The board chose to focus on new markets abroad, while keeping a small product-development project running at home.

The founders did not agree on how to run an overseas business. Yusuf wanted every overseas store to copy Malé exactly: same recipes, same prices, same design, because "that is what made us successful." Aishath argued that each country should be treated as a separate business with its own local manager making all decisions. Hassan wanted something in between: "We should see Southeast Asia as one region with one ICETO brand and one strategy, but let each country adjust flavours and promotions to local tastes." By early 2026, Hassan's view was winning support.

3. The two-part plan

Aishath's years in Singapore gave the founders a first contact in the region. In late 2025, a buyer from 7-Eleven Singapore tasted ICETO at a regional food trade fair and suggested a partnership. 7-Eleven is one of the most familiar convenience-store brands in Southeast Asia. Each country's stores are run by a separate local operator under licence, but the operators share suppliers and ideas. Singapore has several hundred 7-Eleven stores, Malaysia has a large national network, and Thailand has one of the biggest 7-Eleven networks in the world, with stores on almost every busy street.

From these conversations, the founders developed a two-part plan. Part one is a flagship ICETO gelato store in Singapore: a premium, sit-down store in a busy shopping district, serving fresh scoops, gelato desserts and coffee, and designed to show the full ICETO brand experience. It would be owned and run by ICETO through a new Singapore company. Part two is a range of packaged ICETO gelato cups sold in 7-Eleven freezers, starting in Singapore and then rolling out to Malaysia and Thailand if sales targets are met. The flagship would build the brand's image; the 7-Eleven range would bring volume and reach.

Hassan explains the logic: "The flagship tells people who we are. 7-Eleven puts us in their hand every day." Aishath is less sure. "If people can buy ICETO for a few dollars in a convenience store, why would they pay three times more at our flagship? We could end up confusing our own customers."

4. How to enter: the options

For the flagship, the founders considered three routes. They could open it themselves through a wholly-owned Singapore company, giving full control over design, staff and service but requiring the largest investment in rent, fit-out and hiring. They could franchise it to a Singapore food-and-beverage group, which would invest the money and run the store while paying ICETO a fee. Or they could form a joint venture with a local café group, sharing the cost, the risk and the decisions. Singapore commercial rents are among the highest in Asia, so the choice matters for ICETO's limited cash.

For the 7-Eleven range, 7-Eleven Singapore made two suggestions. First, rather than ICETO shipping frozen gelato from Malé, it recommends that ICETO use Selatan Frozen Foods, a frozen-dessert manufacturer in Johor, Malaysia, just across the causeway from Singapore. Selatan already makes chilled and frozen products for 7-Eleven operators in Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand, holds halal certification recognised in both Malaysia and Singapore, and is trusted by the 7-Eleven operators for food safety and on-time delivery. Under a contract manufacturing agreement, Selatan would produce ICETO cups to ICETO's recipes, and ICETO's own team would approve every batch. Second, 7-Eleven offered a regional supply agreement covering all three countries, giving ICETO freezer space in exchange for a guaranteed supply and an agreed promotional calendar.

Because Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand are all members of ASEAN, products made in Johor can move between these countries with lower trade barriers than products shipped from the Maldives, which is not an ASEAN member. Aishath still has concerns: "Our gelato is slow-churned in small batches. A large factory may not respect that. And once they have our recipes, what stops them making a copy for someone else?" 5. Three markets, three environments

Singapore is a small, wealthy city-state with one of the highest incomes per person in the world. Its population is multicultural: Chinese, Malay, Indian and a large expatriate community, and English is widely used in business. Consumers are well travelled, enjoy trying new food concepts, and are used to paying premium prices for a quality café experience. Food safety rules are strict and clearly enforced, and halal certification is important for reaching the Malay-Muslim community. Competition for premium desserts is intense, with many international and local gelato shops.

Malaysia is larger, with a Muslim-majority population and lower average incomes than Singapore. Halal certification is essential for reaching mainstream consumers; without it, many shoppers will not consider a food product at all. Consumers are value-conscious, but young urban Malaysians spend readily on treats and trends they discover on social media. Business relationships are built on trust and personal contact, and decisions often take time.

Thailand is mainly Buddhist and has a huge 7-Eleven network that sells a very large volume of snacks and desserts every day. Thai consumers have a strong taste for sweet, tropical flavours such as coconut, mango and pandan, flavours ICETO already uses. Prices in convenience stores are low, and most shoppers are price-sensitive, although major cities such as Bangkok and tourist areas such as Phuket support higher prices. Business culture is high-context: relationships, respect and saving face matter, and partners expect regular personal visits.

Across all three countries, exchange rates can move quickly against the US dollar, and governments can change food labelling and import rules with little notice.

6. What the research showed

Detailed published data on gelato consumption was hard to find, so ICETO combined several methods. First, Yusuf and Hassan spent two weeks visiting Singapore, Kuala Lumpur and Bangkok, standing at 7-Eleven freezers, visiting gelato shops and malls, and watching what people bought and how much they paid. Second, 7-Eleven Singapore shared sales data showing which frozen products sold best and at what times. Third, ICETO commissioned an online survey of 600 young consumers across the three cities. The research pointed to four customer groups:

One: Young urban professionals aged 22 to 35 in Singapore: willing to pay SGD 7 to 9 for a premium café experience, choose places that feel special and look good on social media. Two: Students and young shoppers in all three markets: buy on impulse from convenience stores, want a treat for under SGD 4 (or its local equivalent), and respond to new flavours and limited editions. Three: Families: buy multipacks, very price-sensitive, prefer familiar brands. Four: Tourists in Singapore and Bangkok: looking for something they cannot find at home, and willing to try a new brand once.

Two further findings stood out. Convenience-store shoppers decide in a few seconds, so packaging must explain the product immediately. And while the Maldives is famous across the region as a luxury holiday destination, very few respondents connected it with food. 7-Eleven freezers in all three countries are dominated by mass-market ice-cream brands at low prices, with a small number of imported premium pints at the top. No brand offered tropical, artisan-style gelato at a mid-premium price.

7. Product and brand decisions

The two parts of the plan need different products. The flagship would serve fresh scoops, gelato cakes, desserts and specialty coffee, with the full ICETO menu. The 7-Eleven range would be a sealed 120 ml cup plus a four-pack of mini cups. Packaged gelato needs a longer shelf life than fresh gelato in Malé, so the recipe must be adjusted without losing too much texture.

The founders plan to keep the ICETO name in every market and three signature Maldivian flavours, young coconut, pandan, and tamarind-mango, in both the flagship and the cups. Each country would also receive one local flavour for its 7-Eleven range: kaya toast in Singapore, teh tarik in Malaysia, and Thai tea in Thailand. The flagship would also offer seasonal flavours that are never sold in 7-Eleven, to keep it special.

The team is still divided on the brand story. One idea is to put "Gelato from the Maldives" on every pack and on the flagship's walls, using the country's dream-holiday image. Others worry that a country known for beaches, not food, may confuse shoppers who only have a few seconds at the freezer, and prefer to lead with taste and the tropical flavours, keeping the Maldives as a smaller detail.

8. Pricing across the region: the geocentric plan

In Malé, a single scoop costs MVR 65, around USD 4.20. For the flagship, the founders plan a premium price of SGD 7.50 per scoop, in line with other premium gelato shops in Singapore. The 7-Eleven cups are a harder decision. Converting Maldivian prices directly would make the cups too expensive for convenience-store shoppers in Malaysia and Thailand. Letting each 7-Eleven operator set its own price, on the other hand, could create very different prices across neighbouring countries. Traders might then buy cheap ICETO cups in Thailand and resell them in Singapore, and the brand would mean different things in different places.

Hassan therefore proposes a geocentric approach. ICETO would set one regional price band for the 120 ml cup, roughly USD 2.20 to USD 2.90, and each country would price within that band after considering local incomes, taxes, retailer margins and exchange rates (see Exhibit 3). The cup would sit above mass-market ice cream but well below the flagship scoop and imported premium pints. Because production would be in Johor, most costs would be in Malaysian ringgit, and ASEAN trade rules would keep costs similar across the three countries. The finance team warns that currencies can move sharply, so the band would need regular review.

9. The decision

In March 2026, the board meets in Malé to decide. Should ICETO own the Singapore flagship, franchise it, or share it in a joint venture? Should it accept Selatan as its manufacturer, and how can it protect its recipes and quality? Which customers should the flagship and the 7-Eleven range each target, and can one brand sit comfortably in both a premium café and a convenience-store freezer? And is one regional price band realistic? "This is the biggest decision we have made since opening the first counter," Hassan tells the board. "If we get Singapore right, 7-Eleven can take us across Southeast Asia. If we get it wrong, we may not get a second chance."
